Shalton Village: 1910 vs 2010

Changes in part of a village over 100 years

🏘️ Shalton in 2010

The two maps illustrate how a part of Shalton village changed between 1910 and 2010. Overall, the village underwent significant development over the 100-year period, with many old buildings replaced by new commercial and residential facilities, and a large increase in the number of houses.

In 1910, the area was mainly residential and agricultural, with a school, a butcher, a grocer, a baker, a post office, and a large house surrounded by trees. The main roads, School Road and London Road, had very few buildings along them. By 2010, however, the butcher, grocer, and baker had all been replaced by a supermarket and a parking area on School Road, while a bank was built near the post office.

The most striking change was in housing. In 1910, there were only a few houses, mainly along High Street. By 2010, however, numerous houses had been built along both London Road and the side streets, transforming the area into a much more densely populated residential zone. The large house and the trees in the centre of the village were removed to make way for these new homes.

In addition, a café appeared on High Street in 2010, replacing some of the older buildings. Overall, the maps show a clear shift from a small rural village with local shops to a more modern, urbanised settlement with larger commercial facilities and significantly more housing.
